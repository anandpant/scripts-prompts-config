# Unslop

Write for the reader and the task. Preserve meaning, factual precision, and the user's intended voice.

- Lead with the useful point. Include the context needed to understand it, then stop.
- Use familiar, concrete words. Keep technical terms when they are the accurate words for the audience.
- Remove filler, inflated claims, canned praise, repetitive conclusions, and qualifications that add no information. Preserve uncertainty that changes the conclusion.
- Let sentences connect naturally. Split a sentence when it is hard to follow; do not manufacture a rhythm of short fragments.
- Do not add personality as a separate editing step. Avoid forced quips, cute metaphors, slogan pairs, and invented emotional reactions. Humor should fit the user's brief and arise from the subject.
- Choose prose, bullets, tables, and punctuation for readability. Respect explicit house style, quoted material, code, and identifiers.
- For visuals the user will narrate, keep explanation in speaker notes and put only necessary labels and evidence on screen.

Before sending, check whether each sentence adds meaning and whether the result sounds natural when read aloud. Revise the awkward parts without changing the substance.

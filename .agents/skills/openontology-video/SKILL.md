# Open Ontology video

Ground the story in the current brief, audience, and real examples. Write spoken prose for the intro when useful; default to succinct numbered talking points for the rest unless a full script is requested. Choose length and number of beats from the story rather than a fixed template.

Build a logical sequence that answers the viewer's next question. An objection followed by a demonstration is one useful pattern, not a mandatory ladder. Focus on the learning and the user's intended use of time, with tooling as evidence.

Match the requested voice. Dry, serious treatment of an absurd idea can be funny; do not inject jokes, self-deprecation, punchlines, or a universal sarcastic persona.

Choose diagrams, screenshots, demonstrations, animation, or footage according to what each idea needs. Keep labels and key points succinct; put narration in notes. Slides are visual aids, not the script, and need not share one layout. Link exact examples and inspect the rendered result. For video editing, verify playback, framing, pacing, audio, and exports as applicable. Herdr, Git worktrees, and code reviews are optional; content review is part of the work.
